Washington · Congress · 11 November 2026

House Republicans elect Jim Jordan minority leader by 106 votes to 73.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

House Republicans held a closed-door secret ballot on 10 November and elected Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their leader for the next Congress. Jordan received 106 votes; Rep. Steve Scalise of Louisiana received 73. Jordan has served as chair of the House Judiciary Committee and was a founding member of the House Freedom Caucus. The election determines the party’s top leadership post once the new Congress is seated in January, when Republicans will be in the minority after the midterm results.

In the Senate, Republicans re-elected John Thune of South Dakota as their leader. The House vote occurred on the same day as the procedural referral of the Hegseth impeachment articles. Both events took place while the current Republican majority still controlled the House floor calendar.

What is only a quote

Descriptions of Jordan as a hard-charging or combative leader are characterizations offered by members and commentators. The vote itself is a numerical result of the conference ballot. Statements about how Jordan will approach investigations of the executive branch in the next Congress are predictions, not actions already taken.

Mechanism a reader needs

Each party elects its own floor leader. In the minority the leader is the principal spokesperson and organizer for the party on the floor and in conference. The selection is internal; it does not require a vote of the full House. The current Speaker and majority leader remain in place until the new Congress organizes. Jordan’s selection replaces the prior leadership arrangement for the Republican conference going forward.

The midterm losses reduced the Republican conference size and changed the majority-minority status. Leadership races after such elections often turn on whether members prefer continuity or a different style. The 106-73 margin is the recorded outcome of that preference among the members who will serve in the next House.

What this story is not

It is not a change in the current House majority. It is not an election by the full House. It is not a statement about any specific investigation that has not yet been opened. The photograph is a standard exterior view of the Capitol.
