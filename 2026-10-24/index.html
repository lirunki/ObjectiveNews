On 23 October the U.S. Department of Justice filed suit in Anchorage seeking to block certification of Alaska’s 3 November Senate race if Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan loses by a margin that could be attributed to votes for a second candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan. President Trump said he ordered the filing. The same day the White House announced that First Lady Melania Trump had secured a localized humanitarian ceasefire in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky, scheduled to begin around 29 October, for aid and civilian evacuations. In Spain, with general elections set for 29 November, Pedro Sánchez and Alberto Núñez Feijóo held precampaign events: Sánchez promised to entrench abortion rights in the Constitution; Feijóo said he would not pardon named figures he called corrupt.

What remains unknown: whether any federal judge will grant the Alaska relief before or after the election; the precise start time, geographic box, and security protocol of the Oleshky pause and whether aid actually moves; the final candidate lists and coalition agreements on the Spanish left ahead of the 13 November campaign start; and the actual vote totals in Alaska and Spain on their respective election days.

Department of Justice sues Alaska to block Senate certification if the Republican incumbent loses

The complaint, filed 23 October in Anchorage, names Alaska election officials and asks a court to prevent certification of a Mary Peltola win if the margin is smaller than the votes received by Daniel J. Sullivan, a same-name Republican listed without a party label. Alaska uses ranked-choice voting; the state Supreme Court had already kept the second Sullivan on the ballot. Trump told reporters he directed the department to file. The suit treats potential voter confusion as a violation of the National Voter Registration Act. No court has ruled. The numbers that matter are the final vote totals and any later examination of ballots, neither of which exists yet. The filing does not itself stop the election or remove any candidate.

First Lady Melania Trump’s office says she secured a localized ceasefire in Russian-occupied Oleshky

The White House stated on 23 October that Melania Trump acted as exclusive intermediary for a temporary pause in Oleshky so that humanitarian aid can enter and civilians who wish to leave can do so via a designated corridor. Ukrainian and Russian officials confirmed a limited arrangement. The city, occupied since February 2022 and heavily flooded after the Kakhovka Dam breach, has roughly 1,700–2,000 remaining residents, mostly elderly. The pause is scheduled to begin around 29 October. Implementation details (exact start, geographic limits, security) have not been fully published. The announcement does not change the wider front line or existing U.S. assistance authorities. Whether trucks and people actually move is the observable test.

En precampaña del 29-N, Sánchez promete blindar el aborto en la Constitución y Feijóo anuncia que no indultará a corruptos

El BOE publicó el 6 de octubre el decreto que convoca elecciones para el 29 de noviembre. El 23–24 de octubre Sánchez y Feijóo realizaron actos de precampaña. Sánchez dijo que blindará el derecho al aborto en la Constitución; Feijóo afirmó que “todo se sabrá” y que no indultará a Sánchez, Zapatero, Leire y Ábalos. Estas son declaraciones políticas, no textos legales aprobados. Las encuestas proyectan una mayoría de PP+Vox en muchos escenarios, pero ninguna da mayoría absoluta a un solo partido. El sistema d’Hondt y el reparto provincial (Madrid +1 escaño, Cádiz −1) están fijados. La izquierda a la izquierda del PSOE sigue fragmentada. El calendario oficial no cambia por los discursos.
