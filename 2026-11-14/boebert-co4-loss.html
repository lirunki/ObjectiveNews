United States · Elections · 14 November 2026

Eileen Laubacher defeats Lauren Boebert in Colorado’s 4th Congressional District

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Friday 13 November 2026 the Associated Press and multiple other outlets projected that Democrat Eileen Laubacher defeated Republican incumbent Lauren Boebert in Colorado’s 4th Congressional District. With approximately 99 percent of ballots counted, Laubacher held roughly 48.8 percent of the vote to Boebert’s 47.9 percent, a margin of several thousand votes outside the automatic recount threshold. The district, which includes the Eastern Plains and parts of Douglas County, voted for Donald Trump by 18 percentage points in the 2024 presidential election. Boebert issued a video concession and stated she called Laubacher to congratulate her. Campaign finance disclosures show Laubacher raised approximately $13.7 million to Boebert’s $1.2 million.

What is only a quote

Boebert described the outcome as part of broader Republican losses and said the job does not define her. Laubacher called the result historic and listed priorities including lower costs, healthcare, and rural communities. These statements are candidate characterizations; the vote totals themselves are from county canvass data.

Mechanism and history

Colorado’s 4th District has been held by Republicans for most of the period since the 1970s. Boebert first won the neighboring 3rd District in 2020, survived a close 2022 reelection, then moved to the 4th District for 2024 where she won more comfortably. Midterm elections in 2026 produced Democratic gains in multiple competitive and previously safe Republican seats. Final certification of the Colorado results will occur under state law; no recount has been reported as triggered by the current margin.

What the story is not

This is not a statewide or national election result. It is one House seat. It does not by itself determine control of the chamber, which depends on the full set of 435 races. Characterizations of the candidate’s personal history or media profile are secondary to the certified vote count.
