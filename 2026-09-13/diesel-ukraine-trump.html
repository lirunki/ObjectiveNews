Energy · 13 September 2026

What is known about record U.S. diesel and Trump’s call for Ukraine to spare Russian refineries

Neutral reconstruction from wire reporting. Not an endorsement of any official’s causal story.

On Thursday 10 September 2026, price trackers recorded the first national U.S. retail diesel average at or above $6 a gallon. GasBuddy put the print at $6.00; AAA’s Friday reading was $6.0556. A year earlier the AAA average was about $3.71. Twenty-eight states set records. U.S. distillate inventories were reported by EIA around 13% below the five-year average. Brent had settled above $107 and WTI above $102. Those pump and inventory numbers are not in dispute among the major trackers.

Approximate U.S. retail diesel (AAA / GasBuddy)

Schematic from AAA and GasBuddy prints quoted by Reuters and Bloomberg, 10–11 Sep 2026.

On Sunday 13 September at the Irish Open in Doonbeg, President Trump said Volodymyr Zelenskyy “has to stop knocking out diesel fuel in Russia,” that Washington had already raised the point, and that “there are plenty of other targets.” He said the shortage “isn’t done by the Middle East, this is done by what’s happening with Russia and Ukraine.” Reuters, AP and POLITICO Europe carried matching quotations.

Two supply shocks are visible at once. Ukrainian long-range strikes have hit a large set of Russian refining assets (weekend tallies: more than 60 strikes on at least 24 refineries). Russia restricted diesel exports. Ukraine says refineries that fund an invasion are lawful targets. Separately, the U.S.–Iran war has cut Middle East refining and disrupted the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters’ 10 September diesel story named both wars. A European official quoted by POLITICO said Trump was using Kyiv as a scapegoat.

A truthful minimum: diesel is at a record; both wars remove product; U.S. inventories were lean; Trump assigns sole public blame to Ukraine’s refinery campaign; Ukraine and several outside officials reject that assignment. Pump data are findings. “Hurting the world” / “not the Middle East” are presidential claims.
