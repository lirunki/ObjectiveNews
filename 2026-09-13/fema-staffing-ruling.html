U.S. courts · 12–13 September 2026

A federal judge held that DHS may not order FEMA to cut half its staff

Based on AP, Reuters and the district court’s published reasoning as quoted in those wires.

Late Friday 11 September 2026 (reported Saturday–Sunday), U.S. District Judge Susan Illston of the Northern District of California ruled that DHS acted unlawfully when it directed FEMA toward a 50% staffing cut and when it pulled FEMA’s power to renew on-call disaster employees (CORE).

The legal hook is post-Katrina statute: DHS “may not substantially or significantly reduce” FEMA’s authorities, responsibilities or functions. Illston wrote that DHS “unlawfully usurped” that authority and that the 50% target “appears as if pulled from thin air.” Reuters reported a projected 11,383 employees—about half of prior levels—without a reasoned record. The Friday opinion does not itself set a full remedy. The administration did not complete a 50% cut; headcount had already fallen. A related decision faulted officials for Signal messages on personal phones that were deleted.

DHS had not issued a substantial public reply by Sunday morning. Illston is a Clinton appointee—relevant to political reception, irrelevant to whether the quoted statute exists.
