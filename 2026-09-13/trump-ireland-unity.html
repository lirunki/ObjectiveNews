Diplomacy · 12–13 September 2026

Trump said Irish unification would be “fantastic.” That is a comment, not a change in the Good Friday rules.

Quotations from Reuters, BBC and Irish Times. The 1998 Agreement is not amended by a U.S. remark.

After talks at Farmleigh on 12 September, Trump was asked about a united Ireland. Reuters recorded: he would “love to see it unified,” that “it’s going to happen eventually,” and that it would be “a fantastic thing.” He gestured at Martin as “the right man… to get it moving,” added that “the UK will have something to say,” and later called unification “a very cool thing.” On Sunday in Clare he called Saturday’s answer “a fairly routine comment” and said putting Ireland and Northern Ireland “together” seemed natural.

Those sentences break with recent U.S. practice of not endorsing an outcome. They do not rewrite the Good Friday Agreement. A unity poll is a U.K. Secretary of State decision if a Northern Ireland majority looks likely. Downing Street said a referendum remains off the table. A Taoiseach spokesperson said Irish policy had not changed. Unionists dismissed the remarks. Dublin saw a “No Welcome for Trump” march. Trump also said Ireland had “fleeced” the U.S. on corporate location and criticized European immigration policy.

Complicity risk: treating “it’s going to happen eventually” as an evidenced forecast, or “the right man” as an Irish government program. Martin did not announce a campaign.
