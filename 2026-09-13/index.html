Record U.S. diesel and Trump’s demand that Ukraine stop striking Russian refineries

Technology · Markets · Congress

AI chiefs call for a slower frontier; OpenAI rules out a 2026 IPO

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a long essay Saturday arguing that capability growth is outrunning safety work and proposing third-party evaluators, industry speed limits, and eventual great-power talks. Sam Altman and Elon Musk said they agreed in principle. Altman separately told Fortune that OpenAI will not list in 2026 because “right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public.” House Speaker Mike Johnson said AI executives should brief Congress but resisted a development moratorium; President Trump dismissed catastrophe scenarios as “things that won’t happen.”

Courts · Disaster agency

Judge: DHS plan to cut FEMA staff by 50% was unlawful

U.S. District Judge Susan Illston (N.D. Cal.) held late Friday that the Department of Homeland Security unlawfully directed FEMA toward a 50% workforce cut and stripped the agency of personnel authority that post-Katrina law reserved to FEMA. She called the 50% figure as if “pulled from thin air.” The ruling does not itself restore every job; the full 50% cut was never completed. DHS had not issued a detailed public response by Sunday morning.

In Dublin and Doonbeg, Trump says Irish unification would be “fantastic”

España · Migración

Ceuta: el Gobierno cifra 5.321 retornos a Marruecos; Ceuta habla de miles aún en la ciudad

El ministro Ángel Víctor Torres anunció el domingo que 5.321 personas han regresado voluntariamente a Marruecos desde el 10 de agosto, unas 90 han renunciado al asilo, y 864 que dormían a la intemperie fueron realojadas. El Estado habla de 4.500 plazas más 2.000 menores a cargo de Ceuta. El gobierno local estima alrededor de 13.000 personas todavía en la ciudad. La avalancha original (30–31 de julio) se midió en decenas de miles; la mayoría cruzó de vuelta en horas. Hay disputa política sobre si Madrid tuvo avisos previos.
