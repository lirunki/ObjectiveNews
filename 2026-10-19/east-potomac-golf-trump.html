Washington, D.C. · parks · 19 October 2026

President Trump announced a plan to close East Potomac Golf Links for 15 months starting 15 November.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Sunday, 18 October, President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social that the Department of the Interior, under Secretary Doug Burgum and with golf course architect Tom Fazio, had developed a plan to transform the existing East Potomac Golf Links into a “World Class Championship Golf Course.” He stated that restoration work would begin on 15 November and that the course would reopen in approximately 15 months. Trump wrote that the completed course would remain open to the public while being designed to host major events such as the U.S. Open, PGA Championship, Ryder Cup, and Presidents Cup. He described the current course as old, broken, dilapidated, with falling and disease-infested trees and a collapsing seawall, and said that when originally built it was famous for open views of Washington that are now overgrown.

AP, the New York Times, the Washington Post, and other outlets reported the post and the 15 November date. They also reported that a federal judge, Ana Reyes, had previously ordered the administration to present long-term plans and had recently halted further tree removal after National Park Service crews cut hundreds of trees over a holiday weekend, which the judge called inexcusable for lack of notice to the court and to plaintiffs challenging the project. Trump’s post did not acknowledge the pending litigation. The course is a public facility on Hains Point operated under arrangements with the National Park Service. Prior designs and the new rendering differ in layout, with the October version showing a single 18-hole course extending farther into recreational space.

What is only a quote

Trump’s characterizations of the current course as “of no further use or effect” and “unsafe” are the president’s assessment. They are not findings from an independent engineering report published with the announcement. The claim that the course will be able to host major championships is a design aspiration; it is not a commitment by the USGA, PGA of America, or Ryder Cup organizers. The assertion that the public will retain access is the stated intent; the final fee structure, tee-time allocation, and any restrictions on non-golf recreation are not specified in the social-media post.

The Interior Department’s earlier statements that tree removal implemented a 2019 Cultural Landscape Report are background. The judge’s order limiting further removal until at least 26 October is a court action, not a permanent injunction against all work.

Mechanism a reader needs

East Potomac Park is federal land administered by the National Park Service. Changes to historic landscapes or significant alterations require compliance with the National Environmental Policy Act, the National Historic Preservation Act, and any court orders entered in pending litigation. The lawsuit by a preservation group and local residents seeks to block a surprise overhaul. A judge’s requirement for prior notice of long-term plans creates a procedural hurdle: the administration can announce a concept, but executing construction that alters the site without satisfying the notice condition risks contempt or further injunctions.

Golf-course redesign by a named architect such as Tom Fazio typically involves grading, drainage, bunker and green construction, irrigation, and clubhouse work. A 15-month closure for a full championship rebuild on a flood-prone peninsula is consistent with the scale of such projects, but the timeline assumes continuous work without legal interruption. Public access after reopening depends on the lease or concession terms ultimately approved. The current low green fees and multiple shorter courses serve a broad local user base; a single championship layout may change that mix even if formally open to the public.

Tree removal of several hundred specimens over a weekend, followed by a judicial halt, illustrates the tension between executive action and judicial oversight of federal land. The seawall condition is a separate infrastructure question; its collapse risk is a maintenance issue that can be addressed without a full championship redesign.

What this story is not

It is not a completed construction contract or an appropriation. It is not a finding that the existing course is structurally unsafe for play. It is not a statement about the quality of Tom Fazio’s other designs. The photograph above is a 2013 type image of the clubhouse. It is not a rendering of the proposed course or a picture of the recent tree removal.
