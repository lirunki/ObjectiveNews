Washington · personnel · 6–7 November 2026

President Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On 6 November 2026 President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that Stephen Miller would become White House chief of staff starting Monday 9 November. Miller had served as deputy chief of staff for policy. Trump said Wiles, the first woman to hold the post, had done an outstanding job and would move to the Trump Political Operation. Wiles posted on X that serving the president had been the honor of a lifetime and that Friday would be her last day in the role. Multiple wire services and major outlets reported the same sequence from the posts and from briefings.

The announcement followed the 3 November midterm elections in which Associated Press and Reuters tallies showed Democrats securing majorities in both the House and the Senate. The personnel change is a recorded decision by the president. It does not, by itself, measure the contribution of any single policy to the election results.

What is only a quote

Trump’s description of Miller as “one of my most trusted advisors” and his statement that “sometimes in life you have to make moves” are the president’s characterizations. Miller’s reply that he was honored and that the mission was clear is his statement. Sources described by outlets as familiar with internal discussions offered explanations ranging from mutual decision to dissatisfaction with the midterm outcome; those accounts are not primary documents and remain attributed rather than independently verified in the public record used here.

Mechanism a reader needs

The White House chief of staff manages the president’s schedule, information flow, and coordination among senior aides and agencies. The post has no statutory authority independent of the president; its influence derives from proximity and the president’s delegation. In recent administrations the role has rotated with shifts in political context or personal dynamics. Wiles had served since the start of the second term. Miller had been a senior domestic-policy adviser with a public profile on immigration enforcement. The transition occurs as a Democratic Congress prepares to take office, which changes the legislative pathway for administration proposals and expands the scope of possible oversight.

Personnel decisions of this type are ordinarily communicated first by the president or the departing and incoming officials. Subsequent reporting fills in timelines from unnamed sources. The checkable core remains the public posts and the date the new chief of staff is to begin.

What this story is not

It is not a finding that the midterms were caused solely by any one policy. It is not evidence that Miller will or will not alter specific executive actions. It is not a biography of either official beyond the roles named in the announcement.
