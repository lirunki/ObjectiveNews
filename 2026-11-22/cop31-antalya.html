Antalya · Climate diplomacy · 9–22 November 2026

COP31 climate talks in Antalya end without agreement after fossil-fuel language dispute

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

The 31st Conference of the Parties to the UNFCCC opened on 9 November 2026 at the Antalya Expo Center in Aksu, Türkiye. Türkiye held the presidency (Murat Kurum); Australia held the presidency of negotiations (Chris Bowen). The scheduled end was 20 November; talks ran into overtime. On 22 November the final draft cover decision was rejected. COP31 President Kurum stated that the deal had not been approved and that no new text would be issued. The European Union, speaking through Ireland’s Darragh O’Brien, rejected the draft because it contained no explicit mention of fossil fuels. Panama and others also objected. Australia’s Bowen described the outcome as “very disappointing.” Multiple outlets reported that oil-producing countries, including Saudi Arabia, had opposed inclusion of phase-out language. The last time a COP failed to adopt a decision was Copenhagen in 2009. The draft had noted that global warming is on course to exceed 1.5 °C. These facts are corroborated by Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera and contemporaneous statements.

What is only a quote

O’Brien’s statement that it is “unconscionable” to have a climate text that does not mention fossil fuels is political framing by the EU. Claims that “some oil states hijacked the talks” are characterizations by EU speakers. Bowen’s assessment of the “geopolitical environment” is his summary. Scientific statements that fossil fuels are the primary driver of warming come from the IPCC assessment reports; they are not new findings of this COP. No country “won” or “lost” in a legal sense; consensus was simply not reached.

Mechanism / history a reader needs

Under the UNFCCC, decisions of the COP require consensus. A cover decision is a high-level political text that can reference prior agreements (Paris Agreement, COP28’s transition-away language) but has no independent binding force beyond what parties implement nationally. The 1.5 °C goal is an aim of the Paris Agreement; the IPCC has reported that current policies put the world on a higher trajectory. Fossil-fuel language has been contested at every recent COP; producers prefer technology-focused or nationally determined approaches, while the EU and small-island states push for explicit phase-down or phase-out. Türkiye and Australia shared the presidency after a diplomatic arrangement. Overtime is common when consensus is blocked. Failure to adopt a cover decision leaves prior decisions in place and means no new collective political signal on the contested points.

What the story is not

It is not the end of the Paris Agreement. It is not a finding that emissions will or will not decline. It is not evidence that any particular country “caused” the failure beyond the documented refusal to accept the draft text. Technical decisions on other agenda items may still have been adopted; the reporting focused on the cover decision.
