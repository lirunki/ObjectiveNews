Seattle-Tacoma · Immigration enforcement · 21–22 November 2026

ICE officers arrest two passengers boarding Alaska flight at Seattle-Tacoma Airport

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On the morning of 21 November 2026, around 8 a.m. local time, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers arrested two people who were boarding Alaska Airlines Flight 1306 from Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA) to Los Angeles. The arrest took place on the jet bridge. The flight was delayed. ICE identified the primary target as Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, a Mexican national who the agency said had overstayed a visa. A second person, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, also described by ICE as a Mexican national and visa overstay, was arrested after the agency said she threw hot coffee at the officers. Video of the confrontation circulated widely on social media. Washington Governor Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, issued statements calling the scene “distressing,” “horrific,” and “chilling,” and said ICE was “completely and totally out of control.” The Port of Seattle stated that the operation occurred without its knowledge or participation and that it was reviewing whether previously agreed protocols had been followed. Alaska Airlines described the scene as “distressing” for passengers and employees. Multiple wire services and local outlets reported the same core sequence: targeted arrest, second arrest after alleged assault on officers, delay, and political reaction.

What is only a quote

ICE’s account, posted on its official X account and in statements to media, asserts that Mendoza “overstayed his visa in violation of our nation’s laws” and that Mejia “hurled hot coffee on the officers” and would be charged with assaulting a federal officer. These are claims by the enforcement agency; they have not been independently verified by court documents available at the time of writing. A woman in the video is heard saying her daughter has a work permit; that statement is a claim by the speaker, not an established fact. Governor Ferguson’s characterization of the tactics as “un-American” is political speech. The Port’s statement that it is checking protocols is a process description, not a finding that protocols were breached.

Mechanism / history a reader needs

ICE conducts both at-large and targeted enforcement of civil immigration violations, including visa overstays. Airport arrests have increased as part of broader interior enforcement. Jet-bridge operations are uncommon because they occur after security screening and in a confined space with many bystanders. Federal law criminalizes assault on a federal officer; the coffee-throwing allegation, if proven, would convert a civil immigration matter into a criminal one for the second individual. Washington is a sanctuary jurisdiction in various respects; local officials have limited authority over federal immigration enforcement on airport property controlled by the Port of Seattle, a municipal corporation. Prior agreements between the Port and DHS on notification or coordination for operations at SEA exist according to the Port’s own statement, but the text of those protocols is not public in the immediate reporting. Flight delays from law-enforcement actions are operational facts recorded by the airline and flight-tracking services.

What the story is not

This is not a finding that either person was or was not removable, that the coffee was or was not thrown, or that protocols were or were not violated. Those questions await possible criminal or administrative proceedings and any internal review by the Port or DHS. It is not evidence of a general policy of arresting every passenger with an expired visa at the gate. It is one documented operation on one flight on one morning.
