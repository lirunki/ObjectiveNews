Washington · legislation · 20 November 2026

The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act by 62-33.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On 19 November the Senate approved the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act by a vote of 62 to 33. Fourteen Democrats and one independent joined most Republicans. Two Republicans voted no. The bill shortens statutory deadlines for environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act and related statutes, expands federal authority over certain interstate transmission lines, and requires large new data centers to pay incremental transmission costs. It now goes to the House. Reuters, Roll Call, CBS, NBC and the offices of co-sponsors Capito, Whitehouse, Lee and Heinrich match on the vote total and the core mechanisms. The text has not yet been enrolled as law.

What is only a quote

Statements that the bill “will lower electricity bills by a specific dollar amount” or “create over one hundred thousand jobs” are projections from outside groups or from sponsors. They are not measured outcomes. Claims that it “solves affordability” or that it “weakens environmental protections beyond recognition” are characterizations. The recorded vote and the statutory changes described in the text are the checkable items.

Mechanism a reader needs

Federal permitting for major energy and infrastructure projects involves multiple statutes. NEPA requires agencies to prepare environmental assessments or impact statements. Reviews can take years and produce lengthy documents. Litigation frequently follows. The bill sets tighter clocks on those reviews and limits certain avenues for later revocation of already-issued permits. Transmission provisions aim to ease construction of high-voltage lines that move power from remote generation to load centers. Data-center provisions respond to recent rate increases attributed in part to new large loads. Passage in the Senate does not enact the law. The House must pass an identical or reconcilable text, and the president must sign it. Midterm election results that shift chamber control take effect in January; the current Congress remains in session until then.

Previous attempts at comprehensive permitting reform stalled over disagreements about fossil-fuel versus renewable priorities and about the scope of judicial review. The 62-33 margin reflects a compromise that attracted enough Democratic votes to clear the 60-vote threshold while retaining most Republican support. Independent analyses of the final text will determine how large any future effect on generation, transmission or household rates actually is. Those analyses have not yet appeared in final form.

What this story is not

It is not a completed statute. It is not evidence that any particular power plant or transmission line will be built on a given date. It is not a finding about the net carbon impact of the resulting projects. The photograph is the Capitol exterior, not the Senate chamber during the vote.
