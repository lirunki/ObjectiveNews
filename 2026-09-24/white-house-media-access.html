Washington · courts and press · 24 September 2026

Judge Kelly issued a 14-day order restoring CNN, MS NOW and POLITICO White House hard passes.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

President Donald Trump announced on 18 September 2026 that CNN, MS NOW and POLITICO would be barred from the White House complex. Reporters from those organizations were later denied entry; some hard passes were deactivated or confiscated. The three organizations filed a joint lawsuit. Early on 24 September, U.S. District Judge Timothy J. Kelly of the District of Columbia granted a temporary restraining order directing the administration to immediately return, reinstate, and restore the hard-pass credentials held by employees of the three outlets that were revoked on or about 18 September, until further court order or the expiration of the TRO. The TRO is written to last 14 days.

Kelly is a Trump appointee from 2017. That biographical fact is not a substitute for the legal holding. He wrote that the plaintiffs had met the high bar for emergency relief, at least on a Fifth Amendment procedural due-process theory. The government, he noted, does not dispute that the journalists received no notice and no opportunity to be heard before the passes were taken. He cited longstanding D.C. Circuit precedent that a White House hard pass, once issued, cannot be withdrawn arbitrarily without process.

The administration later offered national-security language and pointed to specific stories. Kelly called the cited reporting routine on the present record and said he was skeptical that safeguarding national security was the actual motivation. Those sentences are the court’s characterization of the record, not a completed trial finding.

What is only a quote

Trump’s social-media description of the outlets as fake news is a political accusation. It is not evidence that any named article is false. The outlets’ claim that the ban is a blatant First Amendment violation is a litigation position. Kelly’s order rests more firmly, for now, on due process than on a completed retaliation holding. Later on 24 September, reporters from the three outlets said they were still blocked at the complex hours after the order issued. That is an operational claim about compliance. It does not rewrite the TRO.

Mechanism a reader needs

A White House hard pass is a standing credential that lets a journalist enter the complex without a day-by-day escort. D.C. Circuit cases from the 1970s onward treat withdrawal of an already-issued pass as a deprivation that requires notice, standards and a chance to respond. The 2018 Jim Acosta litigation, also before Judge Kelly, applied that framework. A temporary restraining order is not a permanent injunction. It freezes the status quo for a short clock so the court can hear more. The government can appeal. None of those next steps is the same as dissolving the order.

National-security labels in press-access fights are common and often thin. If an outlet published sensitive construction details, the government has tools that do not require taking every badge from every employee of that company. Kelly’s skepticism tracks that mismatch: old stories, public documents, and a sudden ban announced in political language.

What the story is not

This is not a finding that every CNN, MS NOW or POLITICO story in 2026 was accurate. This is not proof that the White House has no authority to set building-security rules. It is a ruling that, on this record, the method used—revocation without notice, justified after the fact—likely fails the process requirement. Historical comparison to other democracies’ press-pool fights is optional context. The operational fact for 24 September is the text of the TRO and the subsequent reports of continued exclusion.
