United States · press · 13–14 October 2026

A federal judge extended the order restoring White House access for CNN, MS NOW, and Politico.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Tuesday, 13 October 2026, US District Judge Timothy Kelly issued a preliminary injunction that continues White House hard-pass access for journalists from CNN, MS NOW, and Politico while their lawsuit proceeds. Kelly, appointed by President Trump in 2017, wrote that the outlets are likely to succeed on claims that the September ban violated due process under the Fifth Amendment and constituted First Amendment retaliation and viewpoint discrimination. He found “just about no evidence” that national-security concerns motivated the ban. The prior temporary restraining order was set to expire that day. Reuters, Politico, CBS, and USA Today matched the ruling and the judge’s key holdings.

What is only a quote

The president’s public remarks that the outlets produced “fake news” or biased reporting are the basis the judge used to infer motive. Those remarks are statements of opinion about coverage, not findings that any particular story was false. The judge’s note that “no one should read this decision as suggesting that President Trump’s complaints are meritless” is a judicial observation, not a media critique.

Mechanism a reader needs

Hard passes are credentials that allow reporters regular access to the White House complex. Their revocation is an executive action. D.C. Circuit precedent requires notice and an opportunity to contest a revocation when the pass is used for newsgathering. Viewpoint discrimination against protected speech is subject to strict scrutiny. A preliminary injunction preserves the status quo while the case is decided on the merits; it is not a final judgment. The administration can appeal.

The outlets sued on 21 September after the president announced the ban on 18 September. The judge’s earlier temporary restraining order restored access within days. The new order extends that restoration indefinitely pending final resolution or an appellate stay. Access is a practical matter of daily reporting; the legal questions are about process and motive.

What this story is not

It is not a ruling that the outlets’ reporting is accurate. It is not a finding that the president may never restrict access for legitimate security reasons.
