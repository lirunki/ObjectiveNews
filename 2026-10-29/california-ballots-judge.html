California · elections · 29 October 2026

Federal judge denies DOJ request to segregate roughly 20,000 California mail ballots.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What the wires agree on

U.S. District Judge David O. Carter denied an emergency request by the Department of Justice to segregate approximately 20,000 mail ballots in California that the department said matched records of noncitizens. The judge concluded that the government had not shown a likelihood of success on the merits or the irreparable harm required for preliminary relief so close to Election Day. California election officials stated that database matches of this kind frequently produce false positives because of incomplete or outdated records. No court has issued a final determination of the citizenship status of the voters on the list.

Reuters, the Associated Press, and the Los Angeles Times all reported the denial of the emergency motion and the approximate size of the list. The DOJ filing itself is a public court document; the specific matching methodology is described in that filing and in the state’s response.

What is a claim or still unresolved

Whether any of the 20,000 ballots were cast by noncitizens has not been adjudicated. Database matches are not proof of noncitizenship; they are starting points for investigation. The judge’s order is a denial of an emergency segregation request, not a ruling on the underlying merits of the DOJ’s theory. Subsequent verification by election officials or by courts could change the factual picture. Claims that the ballots are either all valid or all invalid exceed the evidence currently public.

Mechanism the reader needs

Mail ballots in California are sent to all registered voters. Eligibility to vote in federal elections requires U.S. citizenship. State voter rolls are compiled from multiple sources, including Department of Motor Vehicles data, which can include noncitizens who hold driver licenses. Matching algorithms that cross-reference voter files with citizenship databases produce both true positives and false positives. Segregating ballots before counting is an extraordinary remedy that courts require to be justified by a strong showing of likely success and imminent harm.

Judge Carter sits in the Central District of California. Emergency election-related motions are often heard on shortened schedules. The denial leaves the ballots in the normal counting process unless a later order alters that status. California’s election code and the federal Voting Rights Act and National Voter Registration Act set the legal boundaries for challenges of this type.

Context

Concerns about noncitizen voting have been raised in multiple states in recent cycles. Empirical studies by election administrators and by academic researchers have generally found the incidence to be low relative to total ballots cast, while also documenting that administrative errors and database lags create recurring false matches. The 20,000 figure is the size of the list submitted by the DOJ, not a confirmed count of ineligible ballots. With early voting and mail voting already advanced, any segregation order would have required substantial logistical changes by county election offices.
