El choque de trenes de alta velocidad en Urda eleva el balance a 69 muertos

El Gobierno de Castilla-La Mancha elevó a 69 el número de fallecidos tras el alcance entre un AVE de Renfe y un servicio Iryo detenido en el kilómetro 118 de la línea Madrid-Andalucía, cerca de Urda (Toledo), el 28 de octubre. Las agencias coinciden en que un fallo del sistema de señalización LZB y una instrucción del centro de control de Atocha autorizaron al AVE a continuar a velocidad normal mientras el Iryo permanecía parado. Había aproximadamente 544 personas a bordo. Es el segundo gran accidente en la misma línea en menos de un año, tras Adamuz. El Gobierno declaró tres días de luto nacional. La investigación técnica y judicial sigue abierta; las grabaciones y el relato del ministro de Transportes son las fuentes principales hasta el informe oficial.

Alaska governor orders investigation into Peltola campaign after spoiler-candidate report

Alaska Governor Mike Dunleavy ordered an investigation into Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola’s campaign after a New York Times report that a man named Dan Sullivan, the same name as the Republican incumbent, filed as a candidate and that Peltola campaign staff may have assisted or been aware of the filing. The governor’s office said the review would examine whether state campaign-finance or candidate-filing rules were violated. President Trump called for prosecution. The New York Times article is the primary source of the detailed allegations; state authorities have not yet published findings. The existence of the second Dan Sullivan on the ballot is confirmed by state filings; the degree of campaign involvement remains under investigation.

Federal judge denies DOJ request to segregate California mail ballots

U.S. District Judge David O. Carter denied a Department of Justice request to segregate approximately 20,000 mail ballots in California that the department alleged may have been cast by noncitizens. The judge found the government had not demonstrated a sufficient likelihood of success on the merits or irreparable harm that would justify the emergency measure before Election Day. The DOJ cited database matches; state officials said most matches are false positives from incomplete records. No final determination of citizenship status for the listed voters has been published by a court or election authority. The ruling is a procedural denial of an emergency request, not a finding that the ballots are valid or invalid.

Hegseth removes Pentagon chief planner as Iran options are reviewed

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the director of the Joint Staff’s J-5 plans and policy directorate, according to multiple reports citing defense officials. The move came as the administration continued to review options for possible renewed military action against Iran after the November midterm elections. No official statement has been issued explaining the removal; one report attributed it to a source saying the planner was not aligned with the secretary’s approach. The existence of ongoing Iran planning is confirmed by prior administration statements; the specific reason for Sylvia’s removal remains unverified by the department.

U.S. real GDP rose 3.4 percent in the third quarter, BEA advance estimate

The Bureau of Economic Analysis released its advance estimate that real gross domestic product increased at an annual rate of 3.4 percent in the third quarter of 2026 (July–September), up from 2.2 percent in the second quarter. Increases in investment, consumer spending, and government spending were partly offset by a decrease in exports; imports rose. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 4.9 percent. The advance figure is subject to revision; the second estimate is scheduled for 25 November. Markets and the White House noted the print exceeded some forecasts. The numbers are the agency’s own release, not a political claim.
