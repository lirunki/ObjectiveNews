Ithaca, New York · Criminal justice · 28–29 September 2026

Tompkins County district attorney reopens criminal investigation into 2024 Cornell fraternity sexual-assault allegations

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten announced that his office is reopening the criminal investigation into allegations of sexual assault at the Chi Phi fraternity house at Cornell in October 2024. The decision follows a civil lawsuit filed in mid-September. Van Houten has stated that the plaintiff’s November 2024 sworn statement to campus police did not allege non-consensual drugging or multi-perpetrator assault; the civil complaint does. Evidence will be presented to a grand jury. No charges have been filed. Cornell supports the reopening, conducted a Title IX process, closed the chapter, and imposed sanctions including expulsions.

What is only a quote or contested

Defense counsel for at least one named individual has stated that no investigative body previously found their client liable as alleged. The civil complaint’s narrative is a set of allegations, not adjudicated findings.

Mechanism and history

Civil and criminal tracks are separate. A 2024 decision not to charge does not bar later reconsideration. Grand-jury presentation is the ordinary next step. Title IX campus proceedings use a different standard and do not determine criminal guilt.

What the story is not

Not a conviction or acquittal. Not a completed grand-jury action. Not a resolution of the civil lawsuit. Competing accounts remain pending further process.
