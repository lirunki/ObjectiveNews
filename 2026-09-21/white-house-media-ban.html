Washington · press access · 18–21 September 2026

CNN, MS NOW and Politico filed a federal lawsuit seeking restoration of White House press access after the administration revoked their credentials.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Friday, 18 September 2026, President Donald Trump announced via social-media post that CNN, MS NOW (the rebranded MSNBC) and Politico were barred from the White House “effective immediately,” citing coverage he described as “fake news.” On Saturday, reporters from each of the three organizations—including CNN’s Betsy Klein, MS NOW’s Akayla Gardner and Politico’s Cheyenne Haslett—were denied entry to the White House grounds and had their press passes seized by Secret Service personnel. Major television networks subsequently suspended their pooled coverage of the president on Monday after CNN was removed from its role as the White House television-pool representative.

On Monday, 21 September, the three outlets issued a joint statement that they had notified the government of their intent to sue and then filed a complaint in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. The complaint seeks a temporary restraining order restoring access while the case proceeds and alleges violations of the First Amendment and due-process protections. Defendants named in coverage include the President, Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, Communications Director Steven Cheung and Secret Service Director Sean Curran. These procedural facts—the announcement, the Saturday denials, the joint statement, and the filing of the lawsuit—are reported uniformly by AP, the outlets themselves, the New York Times, CNN, Bloomberg Law and other national desks.

What is only a quote or remains unresolved

Whether the ban is constitutional, whether the White House press area is a limited public forum subject to viewpoint-neutral rules, and whether the specific reporting cited by the President meets any legal standard for exclusion are questions for the court, not established facts. The administration has defended the action as an “assault on the FAKE NEWS” while stating that it “cherishes” the free press; the outlets describe the ban as a “direct assault on the First Amendment.” Neither characterization is independently checkable beyond the existence of the statements. No final judicial order had issued by the close of 21 September reporting. Precedents such as the 1977 Sherrill v. Knight line of cases are cited by the plaintiffs; the government has not yet filed a full response brief.

Mechanism and history a reader needs

White House press credentials are issued under procedures administered by the Secret Service and the White House press office. Historically, denial or revocation of credentials has been challenged when alleged to be viewpoint-based. The White House Correspondents’ Association and the network pool system exist by custom and agreement rather than by statute; the pool provides shared coverage for events with limited physical space. Removal of one network from the television pool prompted the other networks to suspend participation, a collective action that itself has no statutory basis but rests on professional norms.

Prior administrations of both parties have restricted individual reporters or outlets for security or behavioral reasons; systematic exclusion of named national outlets on the stated ground of unfavorable coverage is rarer and therefore more likely to produce rapid litigation. The complaint relies on the principle that government may not retaliate against protected newsgathering by withholding access to a non-public forum for viewpoint reasons. That legal framing will be tested in the District of Columbia court and, almost certainly, on appeal.

What the story is not

This is not a completed constitutional ruling. It is not a criminal prosecution, not a change in the First Amendment text, and not proof that any particular story published by the three outlets was or was not accurate. It is also not a permanent restructuring of the White House press corps; the temporary restraining order request and the underlying case will determine the immediate and longer-term status of the credentials.
