Austin, Texas · immigration enforcement · 20–21 September 2026

An ICE officer shot a Venezuelan man in the torso during a brief foot pursuit in north Austin.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Sunday, 20 September 2026, shortly before 1 p.m. local time, a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer shot a man once in the torso in north Austin, Texas. Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis told reporters the shooting followed a “small foot pursuit” after a vehicle stop under a highway overpass. The man was transported to a hospital in serious but stable condition. Later that day he was released into federal custody.

Attorney Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch identified the man as Wilber Rafael Garces Perez, age 28, a Venezuelan national. She stated he was performing a DoorDash delivery at the time. Local officials, including the mayor and police chief, said Austin police were not involved in the operation and that they had limited information from federal authorities at the time of the press conference. Protesters gathered near the scene, some carrying flags of Latin American countries and signs opposing ICE presence.

The Department of Homeland Security, through a statement attributed to Homeland Security Investigations, said the person shot was “an illegal alien from Venezuela, who illegally entered our country under the Biden administration and had a final order of removal.” DHS added that the individual was stable and in federal custody pending removal, and that HSI with FBI assistance was leading the investigation.

Texas Department of Public Safety reported receiving a request from ICE around 12:50 p.m. for assistance with a vehicle pursuit of a blue Toyota Corolla; by the time troopers arrived the shooting had already occurred.

What remains only a claim or disputed

Whether Garces Perez was the intended target of an immigration enforcement action is not confirmed by local officials. Lincoln-Goldfinch initially told outlets that her client entered the United States legally and held a valid work permit, and that she believed he had no criminal history and no final removal order. She later told the Associated Press she did not know his current immigration status. DHS’s characterization of illegal entry and a final removal order has not been independently verified in open court records released to the public at the time of this writing.

Councilmember Mike Siegel described the ICE vehicle as having rammed the Toyota and the officer firing multiple times; city officials at the formal press conference did not adopt that level of detail. No body-camera video, radio transcripts, or officer statement has been released publicly. The exact distance, threat assessment, and whether the man was armed or reaching for anything remain unconfirmed by primary sources.

Mechanism and recent context

ICE operates under the Department of Homeland Security. Its Enforcement and Removal Operations and Homeland Security Investigations components conduct arrests of individuals subject to final removal orders and investigate related crimes. Use-of-force policy for ICE follows departmental guidelines that authorize deadly force when an officer has a reasonable belief that the subject poses an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury. Those internal policies are not themselves public evidence of what occurred in any single case.

The Austin incident follows earlier officer-involved shootings by ICE agents in 2026, including a fatal shooting in Houston several weeks prior. The Trump administration has publicly emphasized increased interior enforcement and deportations. Local jurisdictions such as Austin have limited formal cooperation with ICE under existing city policies, which can produce information gaps when federal operations occur inside city limits.

Venezuelan nationals have formed a substantial share of recent encounters at the southwest border and of individuals in removal proceedings. Final removal orders are issued by immigration judges after due process; the existence of such an order is a checkable administrative fact once the A-number or court docket is known. Neither DHS nor the attorney has released the underlying immigration-court documents in this case.

What the story is not

This report does not establish that the officer’s use of force was lawful or unlawful; that determination belongs to the ongoing investigation and any subsequent administrative or criminal review. It does not establish the immigration status of Garces Perez beyond the competing public statements. It does not treat the presence of protesters as evidence about the shooting itself. It does not extrapolate from one incident to the overall efficacy or morality of interior enforcement policy.
