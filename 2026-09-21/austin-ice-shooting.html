Austin, Texas · immigration enforcement · 20–21 September 2026

An ICE officer shot and wounded a 28-year-old Venezuelan man during a Sunday operation in north Austin; the man was later placed in federal custody pending removal.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

Local authorities in Austin stated that a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer shot and wounded a man on Sunday afternoon, 20 September 2026, in the north side of the city near the intersection of Anderson Square and U.S. 183 (Research Boulevard). Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis said city officers were not involved in the operation or the shooting and that a small foot pursuit preceded the gunfire. Austin-Travis County EMS Chief Robert Luckritz reported that the man suffered one gunshot wound to the torso and was transported in serious but stable condition to Dell Seton Medical Center.

An immigration attorney, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, identified the wounded man as Wilber Rafael Garces Perez (also rendered Wilbur or Garcés Pérez in some accounts), a 28-year-old Venezuelan national. She stated that he had been making a DoorDash delivery at the time. Hospital staff released him to federal custody later the same afternoon; DHS confirmed he was in stable condition and in federal custody pending removal. Protesters gathered at the scene into the evening. Austin Mayor Kirk Watson and other city officials publicly criticized the federal action and called for independent scrutiny.

The Department of Homeland Security issued a statement that Homeland Security Investigations, with FBI assistance, is leading the investigation into the officer-involved shooting of “an illegal alien from Venezuela, who illegally entered our country under the Biden administration and had a final order of removal.” DHS further stated the man’s condition was stable and he was in federal custody pending removal. These points—location, approximate time, single torso wound, identity via counsel, hospital release into ICE custody, and the existence of a federal investigation—are reported consistently by AP, local Austin outlets, NPR affiliates, BBC, and major national papers.

What remains only a claim or is disputed

Whether Garces Perez was actively fleeing federal officers, whether he was the intended target of the stop, the precise sequence inside or beside the vehicle, and the exact immigration history beyond the DHS characterization are not independently verified in open sources as of the close of 21 September reporting. Lincoln-Goldfinch initially indicated she believed he had no criminal history and no final order of removal, then later told the Associated Press she did not know his current status. Some accounts cite federal sources saying he fled after a traffic stop; others emphasize that he was performing a food-delivery job. Video and stills from the scene show bullet damage to a vehicle and a man being treated, but do not themselves establish the legal justification for the use of force.

City officials, including the mayor, characterized the broader pattern of recent ICE operations as chaotic; federal statements frame the shooting as occurring during a lawful enforcement action against a person under a final removal order. Neither characterization is a checkable fact about the Sunday sequence itself. No body-camera or ICE operational video has been released publicly by the investigating agencies at the time of this writing.

Mechanism and recent context a reader needs

ICE and Homeland Security Investigations conduct interior enforcement operations under statutes that authorize arrest of persons subject to removal orders. Use-of-force policy for federal officers is governed by agency guidelines and, ultimately, by Fourth Amendment reasonableness standards. Officer-involved shootings by federal agents are typically investigated by the FBI or a parallel internal process; here DHS has stated that HSI is leading with FBI assistance. Local police in Austin have no operational control over federal agents and, by their own account, were not participants.

The incident occurs against a background of intensified interior enforcement under the second Trump administration. Separate prior shootings involving ICE officers in other Texas cities have produced local political friction; Austin’s mayor explicitly referenced that history when reacting on Sunday. Protesters at the Anderson Square site carried signs and foreign flags and demanded that ICE leave the city—standard tactical language of recent anti-enforcement demonstrations, not a new legal claim.

Immigration status itself is determined by the Executive Office for Immigration Review and by DHS records. A “final order of removal” means an immigration judge or the Board of Immigration Appeals has ordered the person removed and that order is no longer under administrative stay or judicial stay in the ordinary course. Whether such an order existed for Garces Perez is asserted by DHS and contested or left open by counsel; the underlying A-file or EOIR docket is not public.

What the story is not

This is not a completed investigation. No charging decision against the officer, no civil complaint filed by the wounded man, and no public release of the operational plan or body-worn media have occurred. It is not a mass-casualty event, not a cross-border raid, and not a judicial ruling on the legality of the force used. It is also not proof, one way or the other, of the overall efficacy or lawfulness of the administration’s interior-enforcement tempo; those are separate empirical and legal questions that require broader data.
