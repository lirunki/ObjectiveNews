United States · foreign policy · 11 October 2026

Trump said Saturday it was time for Ukraine to get a new president, linking the remark to diesel prices and a Russia supply deal.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Saturday, 10 October, President Donald Trump told reporters outside the White House that “it’s time for Ukraine to get a new president” and that he suggested Ukrainians “get a new leader who can make a deal.” He said President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had rejected many opportunities to end the war and blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries for contributing to high diesel prices. The remarks came one day after Trump announced a diesel-supply arrangement with Russia under which Moscow would deliver hundreds of thousands of tons in the near term. Zelenskyy had criticized the arrangement as providing funds that would support Russia’s war effort. Ukraine has been under martial law since the full-scale invasion in 2022, suspending ordinary elections. Russian strikes in the Zaporizhzhia region were reported to have killed at least 17 people the same day.

Reuters, BBC, and AP align on the substance of Trump’s statements, the timing relative to the diesel announcement, and Zelenskyy’s prior criticism.

What is only a quote

Trump’s assertion that Zelenskyy “could have made many deals and for some reason he never does,” that the war “started due to incompetence,” and that Zelenskyy has “lost 30 percent of your country” with “over 2 million people killed” are the president’s characterizations. They are not independent tallies of territory or casualties. Zelenskyy’s description of the diesel deal as a gift to Putin is likewise a political assessment. Neither statement converts the other into a verified fact about the battlefield or the market.

Mechanism a reader needs

Diesel prices in the United States have risen sharply during 2026, influenced by the disruption of Gulf shipping linked to the Iran conflict and by reduced Russian refined-product exports under earlier sanctions. Ukrainian long-range strikes on Russian refining capacity have further constrained global diesel supply. A bilateral arrangement that temporarily relaxes sanctions on Russian diesel is therefore a market intervention aimed at U.S. consumers and farmers ahead of the November midterms. Whether the announced volumes materialize depends on Russian production, shipping availability, and any subsequent policy reversal—none of which are settled by the Saturday remarks.

Ukrainian constitutional and legal practice suspends elections under martial law. Replacing a wartime president would require either the end of martial law or an extraordinary political process not described in the current reports. The practical effect of the public call is therefore diplomatic pressure rather than an immediate legal mechanism.

What this story is not

It is not a confirmation that Russian diesel has already reached U.S. ports or lowered retail prices. It is not a statement that Ukraine’s leadership has formally rejected any specific peace proposal. The photograph is the White House exterior, not the event.
