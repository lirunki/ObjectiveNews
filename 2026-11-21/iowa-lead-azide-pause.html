Iowa · environment · 21 November 2026

Eighth Circuit stays EPA permit for Army detonation of lead azide in Iowa.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

Late on 19 November 2026 the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit issued a one-sentence administrative stay of the EPA emergency permit that authorized the U.S. Army to detonate roughly 68 barrels of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. The stay remains in place until the court rules on the emergency motion for a longer stay filed by the Sierra Club and the Iowa Environmental Council. The Army had already announced on the evening of 19 November that it was postponing the planned 20 November blasts until 23 November, citing a forecast temperature drop that could further destabilize the material. Des Moines Register, CNN, WHO Des Moines, and Army statements from Acting Secretary Adam Telle match on the quantity (approximately 9,500–9,700 pounds), the location, the prior postponement, and the existence of the stay.

The EPA issued the emergency RCRA permit on 13 November. The Army said an inspection earlier in 2026 found the lead azide had recrystallized and become too sensitive to move safely. Open detonation in place was selected as the disposal method. About 55 homes near the plant were asked to evacuate for the original Friday window. Illinois and Iowa officials raised concerns about notice and potential lead dust.

What is only a quote

Acting Army Secretary Telle wrote that “public safety is the Army’s top priority.” Environmental attorneys said the permit was arbitrary and that alternatives were not adequately considered. Those are positions. The checkable facts are the permit date, the stay order, the scheduled postponement, and the stated quantity of material.

Mechanism a reader needs

Lead azide is a primary explosive used in detonators. When its protective liquid bath evaporates or the material recrystallizes, its sensitivity to friction and shock increases. Transport then carries a higher risk of accidental detonation. Controlled open burning or detonation is a standard military method for unstable munitions when movement is judged too hazardous. The environmental concern is the release of lead particles into air and soil. Modeling cited by officials projected a plume of limited width; local residents and neighboring states questioned the notice period and the adequacy of monitoring plans.

What this story is not

It is not a final ruling that the detonation is permanently blocked. The court has only stayed the permit while it considers the emergency motion. It is not a finding that the Army’s safety assessment is wrong or right. The photograph is a type courthouse exterior used for visual context of the judicial process, not the plant.
