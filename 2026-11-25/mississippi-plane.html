Mississippi · aviation · 25 November 2026

All six people aboard a Cessna 210 died after it crashed into the water northeast of Horn Island, Mississippi.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

The U.S. Coast Guard reported that a Cessna 210 departed New Orleans Lakefront Airport on the afternoon of 24 November 2026 bound for Page Field in Fort Myers, Florida. Flight-tracking data showed a rapid descent at roughly 10,000 feet per minute before contact was lost. Divers from the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources located the remains of all six people inside the fuselage approximately two nautical miles northeast of Horn Island. The Jackson County coroner identified the victims as Brent Stokes, 42, pilot; his son Hunter Stokes, 16; Arlyn Knox, 40; Michael Knox, 40; and their sons Charles Holden Knox, 12, and Truett Knox, 10, all from the Fort Myers area. The search ended after about four hours. The NTSB is leading the investigation with FAA assistance. No probable cause has been released.

What is only a quote

Any speculation about weather, mechanical failure, or pilot action remains outside the published factual timeline. The Coast Guard’s description of the descent rate is based on FAA radar data, not a final NTSB reconstruction.

Mechanism a reader needs

A Cessna 210 is a single-engine, high-performance piston aircraft with retractable gear and a pressurized or non-pressurized cabin depending on the model. A descent rate of 10,000 feet per minute indicates a near-vertical or steeply diving trajectory inconsistent with normal flight. Recovery of the fuselage with occupants still inside is consistent with a high-speed water impact. Horn Island is a barrier island off the Mississippi Gulf Coast near Pascagoula. The NTSB process typically examines wreckage, engines, maintenance records, weather, and pilot experience; a preliminary report may appear in days, a final report in months. Until that document exists, the operational facts are the departure airport, the tracking descent, the recovery location, and the confirmed identities.

What this story is not

It is not a finding of pilot error or of a specific mechanical failure. It is not an image of the wreck. The photograph above is a type photograph of a different Cessna 210.
