Spain · immigration · 25 November 2026

Kilmar Ábrego García arrived at Madrid-Barajas Airport after voluntarily leaving the United States.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security stated that Kilmar Ábrego García had self-deported to Spain. Wire services and Spanish airport reporting confirmed his arrival on a commercial flight at Madrid-Barajas on 25 November 2026. He was accompanied by his wife, a U.S. citizen, and an attorney. His lawyers described the departure as voluntary and temporary; DHS said he received no incentives and would not be permitted to re-enter the United States. In March 2025 he had been removed to a Salvadoran prison in what officials later called an administrative error, contrary to an earlier court order barring return to El Salvador because of persecution risk. That case had reached the Supreme Court. The current departure ends the immediate removal litigation.

What is only a quote

Descriptions of him as “a free man” or as a symbol of the administration’s crackdown are characterizations by advocacy groups or political speakers. Claims about the precise negotiated terms of the departure beyond the public DHS statement remain unconfirmed by both sides’ full documentation.

Mechanism a reader needs

Voluntary departure is a form of relief under U.S. immigration law that allows a non-citizen to leave the country at his own expense rather than be removed by the government. It does not erase prior unlawful presence for future admissibility purposes unless other relief is granted. Ábrego García entered the United States without inspection years earlier and had been granted withholding of removal to El Salvador. The 2025 deportation violated that order. Spain granted him a tourist visa for up to 90 days; longer-term status is not yet public. The GPS monitor he wore for nearly a year was removed before departure. The operational fact is the commercial flight and the airport arrival; the legal fact is the DHS characterization of self-deportation under a removal order.

What this story is not

It is not a finding that the March 2025 error has been fully remedied in U.S. law. It is not a prediction of where he will reside after the tourist visa. The photograph above is an aerial view of the airport, not the arrival scene.
