United States · foreign aid · 4 November 2026

Trump states that the United States will stop roughly $4 billion in annual funding to Israel by the end of 2026.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On 3 November 2026, during an appearance on Fox News on the day of the midterm elections, President Donald Trump said that he and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had agreed months earlier that the United States would stop providing approximately $4 billion a year to Israel by the end of the current year. Reuters, the Associated Press, and The Times of Israel all reported the substance of the statement. Trump described the amount as money that Israel no longer needs and linked the decision in part to domestic political criticism of the aid.

The same outlets noted that the existing 10-year memorandum of understanding between the United States and Israel, which provides roughly $3.8 billion in military assistance annually, does not expire until 2028. No public document detailing the exact accounts, legal authority, or implementation schedule for an earlier end has been released by the White House or the Israeli government as of 4 November.

What is only a quote

The claim that Netanyahu initiated the idea and that both leaders “made a deal months ago” is a statement by President Trump. Israeli officials have not yet published a confirming statement in the reports reviewed. Descriptions of the aid as a “talking point” used by political opponents are also the president’s characterization. Whether the figure of $4 billion matches a precise line item or is an approximation of the $3.8 billion package is not resolved in the public record available this morning.

Mechanism a reader needs

U.S. military assistance to Israel is authorized by Congress through annual appropriations and multi-year agreements. A memorandum of understanding is a political commitment; changing or terminating the flow of funds typically requires congressional action or an executive decision that stays within existing statutory limits. Ending a multi-year package early would therefore involve coordination among the executive branch, Congress, and the Israeli government. None of those steps has been detailed in the reporting of the announcement.

The timing—Election Day—means the statement entered the news cycle alongside vote counts. Whether it was intended to affect domestic opinion is a question of motive; the verifiable content is the words spoken and the existing aid schedule.

What this story is not

It is not a completed termination of aid. It is not a bilateral treaty text. It is not a photograph of the conversation between the two leaders. The image is a 2024 exterior view of the White House.
