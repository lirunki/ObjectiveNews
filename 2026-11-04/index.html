On 4 November 2026, major U.S. news desks projected that Democrats had secured control of both the House and the Senate following the previous day’s midterm elections, with flips reported in several states that had been Republican-held. Separately, President Trump stated that the United States would stop roughly $4 billion in annual funding to Israel by the end of the year. In Spain, Transport Minister Óscar Puente resigned after the Urda high-speed train collision that killed 69 people; Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez assigned the portfolio on a temporary basis to First Vice-President Carlos Cuerpo.

Still unknown: final certified vote totals in uncalled races, the precise legal mechanism and timeline for ending the Israel aid (the existing memorandum runs through 2028), the full technical findings of the Urda accident investigation, and how Spanish parties will frame the midterm results ahead of their own general election on 29 November.

Democrats projected to take control of both the House and Senate

Multiple outlets, including NBC News and The Guardian, reported that Democrats had crossed the thresholds for majorities in both chambers by early 4 November. Projections cited Senate flips in states including Texas, Ohio, Kansas, and others, and a House margin well above 218. These are media projections based on counted votes and historical patterns; official certification by states and Congress remains ahead. Trump says US will stop about $4 billion in annual aid to Israel by year-end

On 3 November, President Trump told Fox News that he and Prime Minister Netanyahu had agreed months earlier that the United States would stop paying Israel roughly $4 billion a year by the end of 2026. Reuters and AP reported the statement and noted that the existing 10-year memorandum of understanding providing about $3.8 billion annually runs through 2028. The announcement is a presidential statement; the statutory and contractual path to implement it has not been published. The White House image is a type exterior, not an event photo of the call.

Puente dimite y Cuerpo asume de forma transitoria Transportes tras Urda

El 3 de noviembre, Óscar Puente anunció su dimisión como ministro de Transportes durante la Diputación Permanente del Congreso, citando dignidad política tras dos accidentes mortales de alta velocidad en menos de un año, el último en Urda con 69 muertos. Pedro Sánchez aceptó la renuncia y encargó la cartera de forma transitoria a Carlos Cuerpo, vicepresidente primero y ministro de Economía, hasta las elecciones del 29 de noviembre. La investigación técnica del accidente sigue abierta. La fotografía es del edificio del Congreso, no de la comparecencia.
