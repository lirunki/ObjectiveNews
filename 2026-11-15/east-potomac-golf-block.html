Washington, D.C. · parks · 15 November 2026

Federal judge blocks immediate closure of East Potomac Park for planned golf renovation.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes issued a temporary restraining order on Saturday, 14 November 2026, enjoining the federal government from closing East Potomac Park and the East Potomac Golf Course during their normal daily hours. The order runs through 28 November or further court order. It does not require the National Park Service to actively manage golf operations after the recent termination of the course operator’s lease. The park, located on Hains Point along the Potomac River south of the Jefferson Memorial, is managed by the National Park Service as part of the National Mall and Memorial Parks.

President Trump had announced in October that the site would close on 15 November for approximately 15 months to renovate the existing 36 holes into a single championship-level 18-hole course designed by architect Tom Fazio. The administration cited safety, modernization, and aesthetic reasons. Plaintiffs in the ongoing lawsuit filed in February by the D.C. Preservation League and two regular golfers argued that the closure was part of the redesign plan and violated environmental and procedural requirements.

Reyes wrote that the government’s stated reasons for the Sunday closure were pretextual and that the administration had violated at least two federal laws to meet an arbitrary deadline. She noted that the park is enjoyed daily by Washingtonians of all stripes and that an indefinite closure would cause irreparable harm to the plaintiffs. The Justice Department acknowledged that the Park Service can limit access for safety but did not persuade the court that the full closure was justified on that basis alone.

What is only a quote

Trump’s social-media statements describing the future course as “one of the Best and Most Spectacular Golf Courses anywhere in the World” are campaign-style language. They are not engineering specifications or an environmental assessment. Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward’s statement in a Friday hearing that he would be “shocked to learn there is a contract” with Fazio indicates that final design and construction agreements were not yet in place. That is a statement about procurement status, not a finding that the project will or will not proceed.

Mechanism a reader needs

East Potomac Park was created by land reclamation in the early 20th century and has contained public golf since the 1920s–1930s. It is one of the few large public courses inside the District. Closing it for more than a year removes a low-cost recreational option for residents who do not belong to private clubs. The National Environmental Policy Act and related statutes require agencies to evaluate alternatives and impacts before major federal actions. Courts routinely examine whether the agency’s stated purpose matches the record or appears to be reverse-engineered to fit a predetermined outcome. Reyes applied that framework to the 15 November deadline.

Termination of the operator’s lease means the course may lack staffing and maintenance even if the park stays open. The order preserves physical access; it does not guarantee that tee sheets or pro-shop services continue. That distinction matters for both golfers and for any later claim that the park must be closed because it is “unsafe” without an operator.

Tree removal and other preparatory work visible in recent photographs are separate from the full park closure. The order permits ordinary maintenance and safety-related small-scale closures with required notice. Large-scale redesign work remains subject to the underlying injunction and any future environmental review.

What this story is not

It is not a permanent prohibition on renovating the course. It is not a ruling on the merits of a championship redesign versus the existing layout. It is not evidence that any particular tree was or was not hazardous.
