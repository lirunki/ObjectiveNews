Iraq · military · 30 September 2026

The U.S. military said the last American troops left Erbil, completing the Iraq withdrawal.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Wednesday, 30 September 2026, the U.S. military said the last American troops and equipment had completed an orderly departure from Erbil air base in Iraq’s Kurdistan Region. Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell called the move the end of a 12-year coalition campaign against the Islamic State as an organized military force in Iraq. U.S. Central Command said the last forces vacated Erbil Air Base. NPR reported that about 1,500 personnel had remained in the Kurdish region after earlier withdrawals from the rest of Iraq in January 2026. The anti-ISIS coalition headquarters is moving to Jordan while U.S. operations continue in Syria.

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi presided over a handover ceremony and described a new phase defined by Iraqi sovereignty and Iraqi-led security. Baghdad marked the date with public holidays framed as independence from foreign forces. The 30 September deadline had been stated publicly by both governments earlier in 2026, restating a 2024 bilateral agreement to end the Inherent Resolve mission inside Iraq. AP, Reuters, CNN, NPR, and Deutsche Welle match on the date, the Erbil last-base fact, and Parnell’s orderly-departure language.

The presence that began with the 2003 invasion is therefore closed on Iraqi soil as a troop footprint. That is not the same as the end of U.S.–Iraq security cooperation, oil investment, or U.S. activity in neighboring Syria. Wires distinguish those layers. They also record that Iran-backed Iraqi militias remain armed and that Baghdad postponed a disarmament timetable that had been rhetorically tied to the same 30 September date.

What is only a quote

Parnell’s claim that the campaign defeated ISIS as an organized military threat is an official assessment. Residual cells have continued at low intensity; whether organized military threat is the right label is a judgment, not a census. Al-Zaidi’s new-phase language is political framing. Linking the withdrawal to a vacuum that Iran will automatically fill is a hypothesis. Iran already has political, militia, and economic channels in Iraq that predate Wednesday’s last aircraft.

Mechanism a reader needs

Operation Inherent Resolve began in 2014 after Islamic State seized Mosul. The 2011 U.S. withdrawal had already ended the post-2003 occupation once. The 2014 return was invited as a train-advise-assist and strike mission. A 2024 Baghdad–Washington text set a drawdown calendar. Most bases south of the Kurdish region emptied earlier. Erbil was last because it supported both counter-ISIS work and, after 28 February 2026, air and missile defense during the U.S.–Israel war with Iran. Bases there were attacked during that war. Those attacks are a recorded fact. They do not prove that remaining would have been safer than leaving under a published agreement.

Baghdad had also set 30 September as a date for non-state groups to give up weapons. Al-Zaidi last week moved that horizon to 30 June 2027, with a 90-day pause on attacks in exchange for assurances that U.S. forces would not target those groups, then a staged handover. That slip matters more for Iraqi internal monopoly of force than the last American pallet leaving Erbil. CENTCOM’s move of coalition headquarters to Jordan is a geographic shift of staff, not a finding that ISIS is finished in the Euphrates valley. Syria remains a separate legal and military theater.

Readers should keep three clocks: the 2003 invasion, the 2014 return, and the 2024 drawdown text. Wednesday closes the third clock on Iraqi soil. It does not close U.S. activity in Syria or Iraqi militia politics. Counting those as one story produces slogans.

What this story is not

It is not a casualty list for 2003–2011. It is not proof that Islamic State cannot regroup. It is not a verdict on whether the 2003 invasion was justified. The photograph is a type image of Erbil International Airport, a location still, not Wednesday’s departure ceremony and not combat footage.
