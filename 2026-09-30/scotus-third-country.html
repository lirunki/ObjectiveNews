Washington · courts · 29 September 2026

The Supreme Court stayed a Boston order and let third-country deportations resume pending December argument.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On Tuesday, 29 September 2026, the Supreme Court granted the Justice Department’s emergency request to stay a Boston federal order that had blocked the Department of Homeland Security from removing noncitizens to countries other than those named in their removal orders without a chance to argue harm. The unsigned order lets so-called third-country removals resume while the Court hears argument in December and later rules on the merits. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson would have left the lower-court block in place. Reuters, AP, NPR, CNN, and SCOTUSblog agree on those procedural facts.

U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy in Boston had held that the policy failed to give adequate notice and an opportunity to claim fear of torture or persecution in the destination state. The administration told the justices last week that the lower-court order forced cancellation of a flight carrying about 70 people bound for three countries. Rights groups and court records cited by wires put the number of third-country removals since early 2025 above 25,000 people to 29 countries, most to Mexico.

What is only a quote

Solicitor General filings that the Nation does not benefit from delay are advocacy. Trina Realmuto’s statement that the stay allowed sending people where they may face persecution is a prediction about destinations, not a completed harm in each case. White House language that the order affirms lawfulness of the agenda overstates an emergency stay. A stay is not a merits holding.

Mechanism a reader needs

A conventional removal order names a country of citizenship or last habitual residence. If that country will not accept the person, or if the government prefers speed, DHS has sent people to a third state that will take them. The legal fight is about notice and a chance to invoke the Convention Against Torture and related statutes before the plane leaves. Murphy required process. The government said the process collapsed operational flights. The majority did not publish a reasoned opinion explaining the stay.

December argument will ask whether the policy is lawful, whether the district court had jurisdiction to issue nationwide relief, and how CAT and due process apply to a person being sent somewhere they have never lived. Those are separate questions. This is the third time the justices have rejected Murphy’s pauses on this program. Volume to Mexico dominates the 25,000 figure; the legally hardest cases are smaller flights to states with weak asylum systems.

The emergency docket is how the Court manages operational collisions. Readers who treat an unsigned stay as a treatise on non-refoulement are writing ahead of the 2027 opinion.

What this story is not

It is not a ruling that third-country removal is constitutional for all time. It is not a census of who was on the cancelled 70-person flight. The hero image is the Supreme Court building exterior, a location type photo.
