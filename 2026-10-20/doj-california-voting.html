California · elections · 20 October 2026

Justice Department charged 28 lawful permanent residents in California with election-related offenses.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On 19 October 2026, Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter announced that the Justice Department had charged 28 individuals in California with federal election-related offenses. All 28 are lawful permanent residents (green-card holders), not U.S. citizens. The charges include making false statements on naturalization applications and submitting fraudulent voter registrations. Eleven of the 28 had been arrested by the time of the announcement. Officials said 21 of the 28 are alleged to have cast ballots in past elections.

The announcement was framed as part of Operation Protect the Vote and was explicitly linked to a password-protected thumb drive containing approximately 20,000 names that the department had sent to California Secretary of State Shirley Weber and Los Angeles County officials the previous week. The department described those names as people it believes are likely noncitizens who were sent mail-in ballots for the November midterms. California officials have stated they received no supporting evidence for the scale of that list.

AP, the New York Times, and the Los Angeles Times all report the number 28, the green-card status, the 11 arrests, and the connection to the earlier list. The Justice Department has charged roughly 113 people nationwide this year for illegal voting or registration offenses, including about 50 alleged noncitizen voters, against a 2024 electorate of more than 227 million registered voters.

What is only a quote

McCotter said the department “will not look the other way while non-citizens vote in federal elections.” That is a statement of enforcement priority. It is not a finding that any specific ballot changed an election outcome. He also declined to rule out future charges against election workers who processed registrations for people on the list, describing aiding-and-abetting as a “well-established legal doctrine.” That is a legal observation, not a filed charge.

California officials have called the 20,000-name list unsupported and have raised concerns about disenfranchising eligible voters. Those are official responses. They are not court findings.

Mechanism a reader needs

Noncitizen voting in federal elections is a federal crime. Lawful permanent residents may not vote in federal elections. The usual path to a charge is either a false statement on a naturalization form (where the applicant denied prior voting or registration) or a false claim of citizenship on a voter-registration form. Prosecutors must prove knowledge and intent. Administrative errors, such as a person being sent a ballot by mistake and returning it without knowledge of the restriction, are fact-specific and often result in lower-level charges or none at all.

Studies by the Brennan Center, the Heritage Foundation’s database, and state audits consistently find noncitizen voting to be rare relative to the size of the electorate. A 2020 review by the Cato Institute and academic work by Jesse Richman and others have estimated rates well below one percent of votes cast. Those estimates do not preclude individual prosecutions. They do constrain claims that the practice has determined statewide or federal outcomes in recent cycles.

The midterm election is 15 days away. Mail ballots have already been sent in California. Any court order requiring segregation of ballots from people on the 20,000-name list would require a judge to accept the department’s methodology for identifying those names. That methodology has not been published in detail beyond cross-referencing public voter rolls with other government databases and then removing people with evidence of citizenship.

What this story is not

It is not a finding that 20,000 noncitizens voted or will vote in California. It is not a completed criminal case against any of the 28. It is not evidence that the 2024 presidential election or any prior federal election was decided by noncitizen ballots. The seal above is the official insignia of the department that brought the charges. It is not a photograph of an arrest, a polling place, or any individual.
