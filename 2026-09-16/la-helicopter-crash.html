Los Angeles · aviation accident · 15–16 September 2026

A news helicopter covering a Metro bus collision crashed in Chatsworth, killing three people.

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On the evening of Tuesday, 15 September 2026, a single-engine Eurocopter AS350 B2 helicopter operated by Angel City Air for NBC Los Angeles (KNBC) and Telemundo 52 went down between commercial buildings in the Chatsworth neighborhood of the San Fernando Valley. The Los Angeles Fire Department (LAFD) stated that the crash occurred just before 19:00 local time. Three people were pronounced dead at the scene; at least one additional person was transported to a hospital. Fire from the impact damaged four vehicles and two storage containers. More than 70 firefighters responded.

The helicopter had been providing aerial coverage of a separate collision earlier that afternoon between a Los Angeles Metro bus and a passenger vehicle on Nordhoff Street. That earlier collision killed two people and injured others. NBC Los Angeles later identified the two people aboard the helicopter as pilot George Marciniw and aerial photojournalist Eliana Moreno. LAFD officials indicated that one of the three fatalities was a pedestrian on the ground. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) will lead the investigation; NTSB investigators were expected on scene the following morning.

These core facts appear consistently in statements from the LAFD, the station’s own confirmation, and reporting by Reuters, the Associated Press, and the BBC. No official cause has been released. Weather, mechanical condition, airspace coordination among multiple news aircraft, and any possible pilot or systems factors remain subjects for the formal investigation.

What is only a quote or attribution

Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass described herself as “devastated” by the loss of life in both the helicopter crash and the bus collision. Station anchors on air expressed personal loss of contact with the crew before confirmation. These statements are attributions, not independent findings about causation.

Mechanism and context a reader needs

News helicopters in major U.S. markets routinely orbit accident scenes to provide live video. The AS350 series is a common light utility platform used by news operators because of its single-engine performance, visibility, and operating cost. Multiple aircraft from different stations were reported in the area after the bus collision. Coordination of altitude, orbit radius, and communication with air-traffic control is standard procedure; any breakdown in that coordination or any mechanical event would be examined by the NTSB through wreckage examination, flight-data sources if available, and interviews.

The pedestrian fatality underscores that the crash occurred over a developed commercial area rather than open terrain. Ground risk is a known consideration for urban helicopter operations. The NTSB’s preliminary report, when issued, will typically list the basic sequence and any readily observable factors; a final probable-cause report can take many months.

What this story is not

It is not a finding of pilot error, mechanical failure, or airspace conflict. It is not a commentary on news-media practices beyond the factual presence of multiple aircraft at the scene. It is not an assessment of the earlier bus collision’s causes. Claims about the precise number of people aboard or the exact impact sequence beyond the LAFD’s public statements remain subject to confirmation by investigators.
