New York · Immigration enforcement · 10 October 2026

ICE agent shot Oscar Belgal in New York while a 5-year-old was in the car

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

On the afternoon of 8 October, during a targeted enforcement operation in the Marble Hill neighborhood (a section of Manhattan attached to the Bronx), an ICE officer discharged a firearm and wounded a man later identified by city officials as Oscar Belgal, 28, a national of the Dominican Republic. A 5-year-old child was in the vehicle and was not injured. DHS confirmed that an officer fired and that the man was taken to a hospital. NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch stated that plainclothes federal officers approached as Belgal was pulling into a parking space and that shots were fired after the car began to move forward. Seven casings were recovered. Belgal suffered a wound to the neck area and survived.

What is only a claim

ICE officials stated that agents believed their lives were in danger because Belgal “weaponized” the vehicle. City officials and witnesses described a different sequence in which the car was moving slowly or starting to pull forward. No publicly released video of the moment of discharge appears in the coverage surveyed. Claims about Belgal’s prior criminal record are contained in DHS statements; the precise charges and dispositions are not independently detailed in every report. Protests occurred; their size and specific demands are secondary to the shooting facts.

Mechanism and history

ICE enforcement operations are authorized under federal immigration statutes. Use-of-force policies require officers to articulate a threat to life or serious bodily harm before discharging a firearm. Body-worn and vehicle cameras exist in some units; whether they captured this event and whether the footage will be released are open questions. Local police in New York do not participate in civil immigration enforcement under city policy, which is why NYPD responded only after the shots. The presence of a child in the vehicle raises additional operational and public-safety considerations that are not resolved by the initial statements.

What the story is not

It is not a completed investigation or a finding of justified or unjustified force. It is not evidence of a citywide policy change. The image is the agency seal, not a photograph of the scene.
