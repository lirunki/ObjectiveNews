Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO; shares rise 31.5 percent after Trump statements

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

Nvidia Corporation announced on 23 November 2026 that its board of directors had unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer and board director, effective immediately. Huang had resigned on 12 November following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence. The company statement said the board was confident, based on input from senior levels of the United States government, that Huang’s reinstatement posed no national security risk. Nvidia shares closed up 31.5 percent that day, adding roughly $950 billion to $1 trillion in market capitalization and restoring the firm to the position of second-largest U.S. company by market value after Apple. The gain did not fully erase losses since the resignation announcement. Huang issued a statement accepting the role with renewed commitment. The board had publicly indicated only days earlier that it did not intend to reinstate him. Interim leadership had been under Colette Kress. On the same day the U.S. Commerce Department announced preparations to blacklist a Singapore-based Nvidia customer associated with the alleged intelligence-linked individual.

What is only a quote

President Donald Trump told CNBC that he had spoken with board members, “convinced them,” and “made the deal.” He said the board asked for and received assurances, “at least with everything we know,” that Huang was not a national-security threat, and that losing Huang would be the greater risk. Trump’s financial disclosures show holdings in Nvidia stock that could have appreciated by an estimated $1.3 million to $15 million on the day’s move, according to contemporaneous reporting based on the Office of Government Ethics filings (range depending on exact share count as of the latest available disclosure). These are attributions and calculations from public filings; they are not findings of a conflict by any regulatory body. Huang’s own statement does not detail the government discussions. No transcript of the Sunday board call has been released.

Mechanism and history a reader needs

Nvidia designs and sells graphics processing units that dominate training and inference for large artificial-intelligence models. Its market capitalization has fluctuated with AI capital-expenditure cycles. Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and has been its public face and long-time CEO. Export controls on advanced chips to China have been a recurring policy issue; the company has publicly stated it complies with U.S. rules. The resignation followed media reports of personal conduct and the intelligence allegation; the board commissioned an outside law firm investigation whose findings have not been fully published. Reinstatement after eleven days is an abrupt corporate reversal. Stock-price reaction reflects investor assessment of continuity of leadership for product roadmaps (including next-generation accelerators) more than any new technical announcement. Trump’s ownership of the shares is disclosed; any personal gain is a secondary arithmetic consequence of the market move, not evidence of the motive for the intervention. Lawmakers have separately questioned Nvidia’s China-related sales and diversion risks; those inquiries continue independently of the CEO decision.

What the story is not

This is not a finding that Huang committed or did not commit any criminal or national-security violation; the government assurances are as stated, not a completed investigation result. It is not evidence that Trump dictated the board vote; the board issued its own unanimous statement. It is not a collapse or rescue of the AI sector; one day’s 31.5 percent move in one stock does not establish the existence or end of a bubble. It is not a report that Huang has been cleared by any court. Personal-conduct allegations remain outside the checkable public record of the reinstatement announcement. Framing that treats the episode solely as presidential favoritism or solely as market discipline exceeds the documented sequence of statements and price action.
