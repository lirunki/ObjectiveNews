Congress · Ethics · 24 November 2026

House Ethics Committee finds substantial evidence of sexual misconduct by Rep. Jimmy Gomez; he announces resignation effective 1 December

ObjectiveNews. Target T 100 · N 0 · C 100.

What is agreed across desks

The House Committee on Ethics released a report on 23 November 2026 concluding that there is substantial evidence that Representative Jimmy Gomez, Democrat of California’s 34th District, engaged in sexual misconduct toward a House staffer in violation of the Code of Official Conduct. The report states that Gomez made unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact. The staffer, then in her mid-twenties and working in a leadership office, described two incidents in 2024: one at a Washington bar in which she said Gomez put his hand under her skirt and penetrated her, and another in his office in which she said he attempted to kiss her forcefully. The committee found her account credible on the point that the conduct was unwelcome. It noted that the conduct caused significant and ongoing distress and contributed to her decision to leave House employment. Gomez, who lost his reelection bid earlier in November to Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres by a large margin, issued a statement announcing his resignation effective 1 December so that staff could close constituent work. He said he takes responsibility for personal mistakes outside his marriage but disputed the report’s conclusions, claiming key portions of his testimony were omitted and that he had no opportunity to respond. No criminal charges have been filed. The same day the committee released reports on other outgoing members involving campaign-finance issues of lesser severity.

What is only a quote

Gomez’s statement that the report contains “significant deficiencies and omissions” and that his statements were “selectively quoted, disparaged, and disregarded” is his characterization. The committee’s finding of “substantial evidence” is the institutional determination after investigation; it is not a criminal conviction. The staffer’s detailed descriptions of the physical acts are testimony summarized in the report; they are not independently corroborated by third-party witnesses in the public summary. Claims that the process was unfair or that the findings are incomplete remain assertions until any further review occurs.

Mechanism and history a reader needs

The House Ethics Committee is a bipartisan panel that investigates alleged violations of House rules. Its findings can lead to reprimand, censure, or recommendation for expulsion, but resignation removes the member from further institutional discipline. Gomez’s term was already ending because he lost the midterm election. The 1 December effective date allows a brief period for casework transition. Sexual misconduct findings against members have historically led to resignations even when the member’s term was short. The absence of criminal charges means the matter remains within the congressional ethics process rather than the judicial system. The report’s language that the conduct “could constitute criminal misdemeanor sexual abuse” under District of Columbia statutes is a legal characterization by the committee, not a charging decision by a prosecutor.

What the story is not

This is not a criminal conviction or a finding of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. It is not evidence that every allegation in the staffer’s account was proven to the committee’s satisfaction in every detail. It is not a statement about the broader culture of the House beyond the facts of this investigation. It is not related to Gomez’s legislative record or the election result that already ended his tenure. The photograph is an exterior of the Capitol building; it does not depict any person involved.
