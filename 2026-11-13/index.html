Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; Colette Kress named interim

Nvidia’s board announced on 12 November that Jensen Huang resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer and board member effective immediately. Colette Kress, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, was appointed interim CEO while continuing as CFO; the board is searching for a permanent successor and named Harvey C. Jones as its first Chair. The company statement does not detail reasons. Wire reporting and secondary accounts cite earlier reports of personal relationships, including one involving a person described by authorities as a suspected Chinese spy, and a second with a company executive. Shares fell sharply in early trading. Microsoft 365 outage continues into second day affecting Outlook and Teams

Microsoft reported that a widespread outage affecting Microsoft 365 services including Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot began on Thursday and continued into Friday, with partial recovery progress in some regions. The company stated there is no indication of a cyber attack and attributed issues to servers failing automated health checks, leading to elevated load. Downdetector logged peaks near 130,000–140,000 Outlook reports across dozens of countries. Governments, airlines and businesses reported disruptions. Full restoration timeline and complete root-cause details remain pending a post-incident report. The photograph is the exterior of Building 92 on Microsoft’s Redmond campus, a type location image.

One dead, four injured in Logan Circle gym shooting; suspect dies by suicide

Metropolitan Police reported that on Thursday morning Matt Lambert, 55, entered Vida Fitness in the Logan Circle neighborhood, shot and killed building manager James Stuart, 46, then returned to his seventh-floor apartment in the same building and fired from the balcony, wounding four others. After a standoff during which he spoke with negotiators, Lambert died from a self-inflicted gunshot. Police recovered multiple firearms and linked the incident to an ongoing lawsuit and dispute over mold and water damage in his unit. One wounded person was in critical condition. The sequence and motive details come from police statements; independent confirmation of the full timeline is limited. Producer prices rose 0.3 percent in October; 6.5 percent over the year

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 13 November that the Producer Price Index for final demand increased 0.3 percent in October, seasonally adjusted, after a revised 1.4 percent rise in September. On an unadjusted basis the index advanced 6.5 percent for the 12 months ended in October, the largest such gain since November 2022. Goods rose 0.5 percent and services 0.2 percent. The print was slightly below some forecasts. Markets continued to price in a Federal Reserve rate increase at the December meeting. The data are the official statistical release; interpretation of policy implications remains forward-looking. The photograph is a type industrial interior, not linked to any specific producer.
