El informe preliminar de AITAT sitúa el impacto del AVE entre 160 y 200 km/h en Urda

AITAT’s 29 October note gives a preliminary impact-speed range of 160–200 km/h for the Renfe AVE that struck the stopped Iryo service near Urda on 28 October. The LZB failure began around 08:31 and traffic moved to telephone-controlled lateral signaling. The death toll stands at 69 according to regional and AITAT figures; more than 200 were injured. Transport Minister Óscar Puente has ruled out resignation. The investigation remains open; the minister’s “inexplicable order” language is a characterization, not a final causal finding. This is the second major accident on the Madrid–Andalusia high-speed line in 2026.

Justice Department appeals judge’s denial and issues subpoenas in California ballot case

Judge David O. Carter denied the DOJ’s emergency request to segregate roughly 20,000 California mail ballots alleged to come from noncitizens, writing that the relief would “wreak havoc.” The department immediately appealed and has issued grand-jury subpoenas to Los Angeles County election staff for records related to earlier federal letters. The list of 20,000 is based on database matches whose accuracy both sides acknowledge is imperfect. The ruling is a denial of emergency relief, not a determination of any voter’s citizenship. Election Day is 3 November.

Trump cancels planned Kansas and Iowa rallies, shifting to tele-rallies

The RNC announced on 29 October that President Trump would not hold the scheduled in-person rallies in Salina, Kansas, and Dubuque, Iowa. Tele-rallies are planned for candidates in about 19 states. Trump said he did not feel he had to go to Kansas because Senator Roger Marshall was doing well. Polling averages show both Senate races within a point. The cancellations replace travel with virtual appearances in a compressed pre-election window; the effect on turnout is not yet known.

Hackers steal approximately $6.3 billion from Binance in largest known crypto heist

Attackers drained roughly $6.3 billion from Binance wallets across multiple blockchains in about 20 minutes late on 29 October. Approximately $3 billion, mostly stablecoins, has been frozen by issuers. Binance halted withdrawals and said it will cover losses from reserves. Analytics firms TRM, Elliptic and Chainalysis attribute the attack to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group with high confidence. The figure exceeds the prior record (Bybit 2025) by a factor of four. Attribution remains firm-level pending official confirmation; recovery of the non-freezable portion is incomplete.
